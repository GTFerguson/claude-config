The trap in the numbers

Higher F1 scores exist — but every one buys the gain by missing a real goal. Those are a recall trade, not real improvement, and they wouldn't survive the next game's mix of goals and false alarms.

Our rule

Hold recall at 100% as a hard constraint. Any score that costs a goal is a warning sign, not a win — so the gains we keep are real precision, not a lucky swap on one game.

Diligence · closed avenuesTested & ruled out — with evidence05 / 08

What we tried, ruled out, and why

▪ 2nd stage · parked
AI video-verifier

Bolted a vision-language model on top of the detector's firings as a second pass — "did the ball end up in the net?" — to overturn the false alarms. 5 model/prompt variants all stalled at ~0.80: the cage netting fills the frame, so models hallucinate "in the net" for wide shots. The limit is the footage, not the model.

▪ backwards here
"A shot is fast"

Inverted in 5-a-side: real goals are close-range rolls (slow); the false alarms are clearances whipping across the mouth (fast). A speed filter would kill real goals first.

▪ nothing to fix
Trajectory gap-filling

The ball is already tracked through 23–26 of 26 approach frames into every goal. There are no gaps to reconstruct — the data is already dense.

Each dead end is documented with its measured failure mode — so the team never re-spends budget on it. A ruled-out avenue is a result, not a loss.

The opportunity · biggest leverThe accuracy ceiling06 / 08

The ceiling is depth — and it's the biggest jump available

From one flat view, "on the line" and "over the line" look identical. That single ambiguity is the source of the remaining false alarms.

Why the false alarms remain

Keeper saves and just-wide shots cross the goal-mouth region but not the line. A single camera plane can't resolve depth at the line — so they look like goals.

The plan — recover real depth geometry

  • Extrapolate the off-screen goal corners from on-pitch lines (we have prior code for this)
  • Use the perimeter billboard lines as a second geometry cue
  • Bring in the unused interior pitch markings near the goal

The single biggest accuracy jump available.

Shipped · Workstream 02A data system that learns from mistakes07 / 08
Match-anchored ground truth

Every firing — even the false alarms — is now reviewable

The unlock

We could only ever review clips where a goal was already known to happen. The detector's mistakes — the most useful training signal — had nowhere to be filed.

What we changed

Anchored ground truth to the match timeline, so any moment the detector fires — goal or not — can be pulled up, reviewed and added to the training set. Verified labels now survive re-runs instead of being lost each round.

This is the engine behind Workstream 01's next model — it lets the detector train on its own weak spots.
